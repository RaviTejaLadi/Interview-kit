# How do you prioritize tasks when everything seems urgent?

### Definition

This question tests whether you can distinguish between **urgent** and **important** work instead of simply working on whatever appears first.

A practical approach is to consider:

- Business impact
- Production/customer impact
- Dependencies
- Deadlines
- Effort
- Risk

### Sample answer

> “When everything appears urgent, I first try to understand the actual priority instead of treating every task equally.
>
> I look at factors such as production impact, customer impact, deadlines, dependencies, and the consequences of delaying each task.
>
> For example, a production issue affecting customers would generally take priority over a low-impact UI improvement. If two tasks have similar priority, I look at dependencies and deadlines to determine which one should be handled first.
>
> I also communicate the prioritization clearly with my manager or team when there are conflicting priorities. That way, everyone understands what I'm working on and what is being delayed.
>
> My goal is not to work on everything simultaneously, but to make the trade-offs visible and focus on the highest-impact work first.”

### Simple framework

```text
Impact
  ↓
Urgency
  ↓
Deadline
  ↓
Dependencies
  ↓
Effort / Risk
  ↓
Priority
```
