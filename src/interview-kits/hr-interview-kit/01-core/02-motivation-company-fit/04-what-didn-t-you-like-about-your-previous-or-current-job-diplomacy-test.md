# What didn't you like about your previous or current job? (Diplomacy test).

### Definition

This is often a **diplomacy test**.

The interviewer wants to see how you discuss negative experiences.

They are checking whether you:

- Blame people
- Complain excessively
- Speak negatively about previous employers
- Handle disagreements professionally
- Can identify genuine areas for improvement

### ❌ Weak answer

> “Management was terrible and nobody knew what they were doing.”

Even if you genuinely experienced problems, this answer can make you sound difficult to work with.

### ✅ Better answer

> “One challenge was that some projects had changing requirements and priorities, which sometimes made it difficult to plan development work efficiently.
>
> I learned to handle that by clarifying requirements earlier, communicating technical dependencies, and breaking larger tasks into smaller deliverables.
>
> I would have preferred a more predictable planning process, but the experience also helped me become better at dealing with ambiguity.”

### Another example

> “One limitation was that I didn't always have opportunities to work on larger architectural decisions. Most of my responsibilities were focused on feature implementation.
>
> That experience was valuable, but now I'm looking for a role where I can have more involvement in architecture, technical decisions, and system-level improvements.”

Notice the difference:

**Problem → Professional explanation → What you learned → What you want next**
