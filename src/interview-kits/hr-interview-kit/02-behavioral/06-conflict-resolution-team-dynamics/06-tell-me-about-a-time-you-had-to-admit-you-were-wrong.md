# Tell me about a time you had to admit you were wrong.

**Definition:**  
This tests **accountability, self-awareness, and willingness to learn**. Choose an example where you made a genuine mistake but took responsibility and corrected it.

**Sample answer:**

> In one project, I initially believed that a performance issue was caused by unnecessary API requests. I spent some time investigating the network calls and suggested changing the data-fetching logic.
>
> After looking at the React profiler and component render behavior more carefully, I realized that the API wasn't the main problem. The actual issue was unnecessary component re-renders caused by how some props and callbacks were being created.
>
> I told the team that my initial diagnosis was incorrect and explained what I had found. I then worked on the actual issue and verified the improvement.
>
> It was a good reminder that I shouldn't become attached to my first hypothesis. When debugging, I try to let evidence guide the conclusion.

**Strong closing line:**

> "Being wrong isn't the problem; refusing to change your conclusion after getting better evidence is."
