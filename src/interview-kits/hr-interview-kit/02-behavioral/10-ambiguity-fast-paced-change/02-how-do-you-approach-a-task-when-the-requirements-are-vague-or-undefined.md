# How do you approach a task when the requirements are vague or undefined?

**Definition:**  
This tests how you deal with **ambiguity**. They want to know whether you can turn an unclear request into actionable requirements.

**Sample answer:**

> When requirements are vague, I don't immediately start implementing based on assumptions.
>
> First, I try to understand the goal behind the request. I identify what is known, what is unclear, and what decisions need to be made.
>
> For example, if someone asks me to "make the dashboard faster," I would ask questions such as:
>
> - Which part is currently slow?
> - What response time are we targeting?
> - Is the problem frontend rendering, API response time, or both?
> - Which users or workflows are affected?
>
> I then document the assumptions and clarify the important questions with the relevant stakeholder.
>
> If some uncertainty remains, I prefer building a small proof of concept rather than spending a lot of time implementing an assumption that may later change.
>
> My goal is to turn an ambiguous requirement into something that is **specific, measurable, and actionable**.

### A simple framework

```text id="8fbyz5"
Vague Requirement
       ↓
Understand the Goal
       ↓
Identify Unknowns
       ↓
Ask Clarifying Questions
       ↓
Define Acceptance Criteria
       ↓
Implement
       ↓
Validate
```

**Strong interview phrase:**

> "When requirements are unclear, I try to reduce uncertainty before increasing implementation effort."
