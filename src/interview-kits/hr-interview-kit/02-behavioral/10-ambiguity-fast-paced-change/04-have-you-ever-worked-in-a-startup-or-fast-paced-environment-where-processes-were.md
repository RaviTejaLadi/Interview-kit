# Have you ever worked in a startup or fast-paced environment where processes were informal?

**Definition:**  
This question evaluates whether you can operate effectively when there isn't a clearly defined process for everything.

If you **have startup experience**, give a real example.

If you **don't**, don't pretend that you do. You can answer based on a fast-moving project or team.

### Sample answer — if you have this experience

> Yes. I worked in an environment where requirements and priorities could change quickly, and not every process was formally documented.
>
> In that environment, I learned to communicate frequently and make assumptions explicit instead of waiting for every detail to be formally documented.
>
> For example, when a requirement was unclear, I would quickly discuss it with the Product Manager, document the agreed behavior, and start with a small implementation rather than waiting for a complete specification.
>
> At the same time, I didn't want speed to become an excuse for poor engineering. For important areas, we still maintained code reviews, testing, and basic documentation.
>
> I found that the key to working in a fast-paced environment is balancing **speed with enough structure to prevent unnecessary rework**.

### Sample answer — if you haven't worked in a startup

> I haven't worked in a traditional startup environment, but I have worked on projects where priorities changed quickly and processes weren't always formalized.
>
> I handled that by communicating frequently, clarifying priorities, documenting important decisions, and focusing on the highest-impact work first.
>
> I also learned not to wait for perfect requirements before making progress. When something was unclear, I would clarify the important parts and use a small proof of concept when necessary.
>
> At the same time, I tried to maintain engineering fundamentals such as code reviews, testing, and clear ownership.
>
> I think the important skill in a fast-paced environment is being comfortable with change while still maintaining enough discipline to avoid chaos.
