# How do you communicate with stakeholders when a feature is going to be delayed?

**Definition:**  
This tests transparency, expectation management, and your ability to communicate bad news without creating unnecessary confusion.

**Sample answer:**

> I believe delays should be communicated as early as possible. I don't wait until the deadline to tell stakeholders that we're going to miss it.
>
> First, I try to understand the reason for the delay and determine whether the impact is on the entire feature or only specific parts.
>
> Then I communicate three things clearly: **what is delayed, why it is delayed, and what the new options are**.
>
> For example, I might say:
>
> "The feature is currently at risk of being delayed by two days because the API dependency isn't ready. We have two options: we can release the core functionality first and move the remaining functionality to the next release, or we can wait until the complete API is available. My recommendation from an engineering perspective is to release the core functionality first."
>
> I also provide an updated timeline and continue communicating if the situation changes.
>
> My goal is to make the delay predictable rather than surprising.

### A simple stakeholder communication formula

**Status → Reason → Impact → Options → New timeline**

For example:

> **Status:** Feature is delayed by two days.  
> **Reason:** Backend dependency is not ready.  
> **Impact:** The complete workflow cannot be tested yet.  
> **Options:** Partial release or wait for the dependency.  
> **Timeline:** Complete implementation expected by Friday.
